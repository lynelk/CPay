# Internal Audit, Management Review, Nonconformity and Corrective Action

## 1. Assurance principle

The integrated management system is only considered effective when controls operate and produce evidence. A policy, code path or dashboard that has never been exercised is design evidence, not operating-effectiveness evidence.

## 2. Internal audit programme

The IMS lead maintains a risk-based audit programme covering the full scope over a twelve-month cycle. High-risk domains may be sampled more frequently.

Minimum annual coverage includes:

- quality/customer requirement and release processes;
- information-security risk and selected control effectiveness;
- access/privileged-account review;
- vulnerability/dependency/security engineering;
- incident/problem/change/service management;
- backup, restore and business continuity exercises;
- provider/supplier management;
- payment state, ledger, settlement and reconciliation controls;
- privacy/compliance/KYB/KYC and evidence processes;
- financial-message standards/interfaces that are production-enabled;
- ISO 32212 transition-plan evidence when applicable or voluntarily adopted.

Auditors must be sufficiently independent of the activity audited. Where staffing prevents full organizational separation, management documents the independence risk and adds an external or cross-functional reviewer for material findings.

## 3. Audit plan and evidence

Each audit records:

- scope, objective, criteria and period;
- auditor and conflicts/independence assessment;
- systems/processes/locations sampled;
- evidence references and sample sizes;
- conformities, observations and nonconformities;
- severity/risk rationale;
- responsible owner and due date;
- closure and effectiveness evidence.

Evidence is sampled, not copied wholesale into the repository. Restricted evidence remains in controlled storage with an immutable reference.

## 4. Finding classification

- `MAJOR`: systemic failure, absent required process/control, repeated critical failure, serious financial/security/legal risk or inability to achieve the management-system intended outcome.
- `MINOR`: isolated lapse that does not demonstrate systemic breakdown but requires correction.
- `OBSERVATION`: improvement opportunity or emerging weakness not yet a nonconformity.

Critical production/security/financial risks discovered during audit are escalated immediately and are not held until the audit report is finalized.

## 5. Nonconformity and corrective action (CAPA)

A nonconformity record contains:

1. requirement/control and evidence of failure;
2. immediate correction/containment;
3. impact and affected population/period;
4. root-cause analysis proportionate to risk;
5. corrective action addressing the cause;
6. owner and target date;
7. evidence of implementation;
8. independent effectiveness check;
9. closure approver/date.

Repeated incidents, repeated reconciliation exceptions, repeated vulnerable dependency reintroduction or repeated SLO failure must be evaluated as potential systemic nonconformities even when individual tickets were resolved.

## 6. Corrective-action service levels

Default targets, unless a stricter regulatory/contractual/security deadline applies:

- major: containment immediately; corrective plan within 5 business days; completion target within 30 days or approved exception;
- minor: completion within 60 days;
- observation: owner/review date within 90 days when accepted for action.

Exceptions include risk rationale, compensating controls, approver and expiry.

## 7. Management review cadence

An integrated management review occurs at least quarterly, with a formal annual review of the entire scope. The meeting may be combined with an operating/business review only if all required management-system inputs, decisions and records are explicitly covered.

### Required inputs

- prior actions and overdue items;
- changes in internal/external context and interested-party obligations;
- customer/support/complaint trends and quality objectives;
- service/SLO/capacity/availability performance;
- incidents, problems, change failure and release performance;
- information-security objectives, risks, vulnerabilities and significant events;
- privacy/compliance/regulatory changes and cases;
- supplier/provider performance and concentration risk;
- reconciliation, settlement and financial-control exceptions;
- audit/assessment/penetration-test results;
- continuity/DR exercise performance and RTO/RPO evidence;
- resources, competence and training needs;
- status of financial-message certification profiles;
- climate-context and ISO 32212 transition-plan status where applicable;
- opportunities for improvement.

### Required outputs

Management records decisions/actions concerning:

- improvement priorities;
- changes to policy, objectives, risks or controls;
- resource/competence needs;
- service/product/process changes;
- risk acceptance or escalation;
- supplier/provider actions;
- continuity/security investment;
- certification-readiness actions and external-assessment scope.

Actions have owner and due date and are tracked to closure.

## 8. Certification-readiness gate

Before an external certification audit, management confirms:

- controlled scope and policies are approved;
- applicable risks and treatment are current;
- the local SoA is complete for ISO/IEC 27001 scope;
- at least one complete internal-audit cycle has been performed for the certification scope;
- management review has occurred with documented outputs;
- significant nonconformities have corrective plans and no unaccepted critical blockers remain;
- operational records demonstrate controls are operating, not merely documented;
- external/manual legal, personnel, supplier, physical and regulatory evidence is available to the audit team.

The external auditor/certification body determines certification, not this gate.
